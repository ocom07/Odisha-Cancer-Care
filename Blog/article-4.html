Cervical Cancer and the Free HPV Vaccine: What Parents and Women in Odisha Should Know

A plain-language guide to HPV vaccination, cervical screening and symptoms. It explains the national free vaccination offer for eligible 14-year-old girls and why screening still matters.

7 November 2026 · Odisha Cancer Care Editorial Team · Medical review: pending

Most cervical cancers are linked to persistent infection with high-risk types of human papillomavirus (HPV). HPV vaccination can prevent many infections that lead to cervical cancer, while screening can help detect precancerous changes before they become cancer.

Who can receive the free HPV vaccine?

India launched a nationwide free HPV vaccination programme in 2026 for girls aged 14 years, with a voluntary single-dose schedule and parental consent at government health facilities. Availability and session details should be confirmed at the nearest government facility. The free programme should not be assumed to cover every age group.

Does vaccination replace screening?

No. Vaccination does not remove the need for cervical screening later in life. Women should ask a qualified health professional when to begin screening and which test is suitable for their age and medical history. Screening recommendations may differ by programme and clinical circumstances.

Possible symptoms to discuss with a doctor

Cervical cancer may not cause symptoms early. Warning symptoms can include bleeding after sexual intercourse, bleeding between periods or after menopause, unusual or persistent vaginal discharge, and persistent pelvic pain. These symptoms can have many causes, but should be assessed rather than ignored.

Where to ask in Odisha

Ask at a government PHC, CHC, district hospital, Ayushman Arogya Mandir or government medical college about the HPV vaccination session and eligibility. For screening or symptoms, request a consultation with an appropriate clinician. Do not delay an assessment while waiting for a vaccination session.

Important: The 2026 free HPV campaign is targeted at eligible 14-year-old girls. Confirm the current local session and eligibility with a government health facility; vaccination and screening serve different purposes.
