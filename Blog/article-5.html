Breast Lump: Signs, Self-Examination and When to See a Doctor

Understand breast changes to look for, how to become familiar with your breasts and why any new lump or unusual change should be assessed—even if it does not hurt.

7 November 2026 · Odisha Cancer Care Editorial Team · Medical review: pending

Finding a breast lump can be frightening, but not every lump is cancer. Many breast changes are benign. The important step is to arrange a clinical assessment rather than trying to decide the cause yourself.

Changes that deserve attention

Speak to a healthcare professional about a new lump or thickening in the breast or armpit, a change in breast size or shape, skin dimpling or puckering, redness or persistent swelling, a newly inverted nipple, unusual nipple discharge (especially blood-stained), or a persistent change around the nipple.

A simple breast-awareness check

Choose a regular time to notice how your breasts normally look and feel. In front of a mirror, look for changes in shape, skin or nipples with your arms relaxed and then raised. Using the pads of your fingers, gently feel the whole breast and armpit area in a pattern that helps you cover the area. You may do this while lying down or in the shower. This is breast awareness, not a test that can rule out cancer.

When should you see a doctor?

Arrange an appointment for any new or persistent change, whether painful or painless. Do not wait for the next screening appointment if you have noticed a lump. A clinician may recommend an examination, ultrasound, mammography or biopsy depending on your age and findings.

Where to begin in Odisha

You can begin with a nearby government health facility or district hospital and ask about the breast clinic, surgery or oncology referral pathway. Confirm which diagnostic tests and treatment services are available locally before travelling.

Important: A painless lump can still need assessment, and a painful lump is not automatically cancer. Early medical advice is the safest next step.
