Quitting Gutkha, Khaini, Pan Masala and Cigarettes: Help Available in Odisha

A supportive, practical guide to preparing to quit tobacco, handling cravings and finding professional counselling through government tobacco-cessation services.

7 November 2026 · Odisha Cancer Care Editorial Team · Medical review: pending

Quitting tobacco is one of the most important steps you can take for your health. Whether you use gutkha, khaini, cigarettes, bidis or another tobacco product, stopping can be difficult—but support is available, and it is never too late to try.

Make a quit plan

Choose a quit date, tell someone you trust and remove tobacco products from places where you spend time. Notice the situations that trigger cravings—such as tea breaks, meals, stress or social gatherings—and plan a different activity for those moments.

Manage cravings one at a time

Cravings usually rise and pass. Try delaying for a few minutes, drinking water, taking a short walk, brushing your teeth or calling a supportive person. Avoid replacing tobacco with supari or areca nut; these are not safe substitutes.

Ask for professional help

A doctor or tobacco-cessation counsellor can help you choose behavioural support and, when appropriate, medicines to manage nicotine dependence. Ask your district hospital or nearby government health facility about a Tobacco Cessation Centre.

Use the national quitline

The National Tobacco Quit Line lists 1800 112 356 as a toll-free number. The government also lists a missed-call service at 011-22901701. Confirm current service availability when you call. If you relapse, do not treat it as failure—restart your plan and ask for more support.

Important: You do not have to quit alone. Ask a health professional for a plan that fits your tobacco use, health and daily routine.
